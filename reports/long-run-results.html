Part 0

What was tested

The confirmed rule set, two exits, three entry filters, two lists, one start per list.

  • R1 Universe and timing. Stocks of the index (point-in-time monthly lists). One check a day near the close.
  • R2 New stock. Furthest below its 20-day average, not held. No cap on stocks held; only cash limits it (capital / 10 per buy).
  • R3 Size of a buy. Capital / 10; capital = start money plus profit booked, refreshed after every sale.
  • R4 Add. 3% below the latest open lot, at most 3 lots open per stock (1 fresh + 2 adds).
  • R5 Sell: profit. Whole exit: all lots at +5% over average cost. Own targets: each lot at +5% over its own entry.
  • R6 Sell: index. A stock dropped from the index is sold that day, at any price.
  • R7 No stop, no time limit. Nothing else sells a position.
  • R8 Order of the day. Any number of exits first, then at most one purchase a day in all (an add before a new stock).

Strategies: after costs = statutory charges + 10 bps slippage a side. "tax, dated" = after costs and tax at the rates in force on each sale date (open lots taxed as if sold on the last day); "tax, today" = the same with today's rates on every sale. Index: NSE total-return index bought once and sold on the last day, taxed once at the end as a long-term gain (LTCG) with the same tax code as the strategies' lots: exempt before April 2018, 10% above Rs 1 lakh from April 2018, 12.5% above Rs 1.25 lakh from July 2024, plus cess, with the 31 Jan 2018 cost step-up. Example: Nifty 50 over 17.6 years grows Rs 10 lakh to Rs 56.8 lakh gross; the tax is Rs 4.2 lakh, which takes 10.4% a year down to 9.9%. Its "after costs" column is the gross figure (no fees). Sharpe, worst fall, longest time under water and Calmar are measured after costs. The Midcap list is mostly Nifty 50 names (36 of its first 50), so it is not a true Midcap 50 test. Why "after costs" can be higher than "before costs": the four stages are four separate runs. Costs and tax change which stocks get bought and when, and a few stocks held through a crash decide a single run (path luck). A test of this: fixing two tiny bugs (a Rs 216 cash overdraw and a month lookup on the 31st) moved single Nifty 50 runs by up to 5 points a year, but the 5-year medians by under 1 point. That is why section 3 uses many start dates.

TWO SMALL BUGS, FIXED, RERUN
All numbers on this page are after the fixes

R8Order of the dayHoldsIndex exits, then profit exits (any number), then at most one purchase (an add before a new stock). Sale cash can be reused the same day.
Why lag 1 is the headline. The membership file has one row per month-end. The engine's convention (lag 0) applies a month-end list to the whole month it is dated in: on 3821 of 4629 trading days the list used was dated later (on average 15 days, at most 30). Example: the 2015-09-28 row removes NMDC and adds ADANIPORTS; lag 0 acts on 2015-09-01, lag 1 on 2015-10-01. The direct gain from that foresight is small (early index exits gained about 2% of the value sold), but lag 1 is the only version that could have been traded.
One rule to decide. R8 says one purchase a day, but not which add goes first when several held stocks are below their latest lot. The simulator picks the stock whose lot was bought most recently (a stock 4% below beats one 10% below). It decided the day's add on 36 to 117 days in each 17-year run. An alternative, "deepest below its lot first", has not been run.
Data caveats (known, not bugs). Nine Nifty 50 members have no price history and can never be bought (HDFC, CAIRN, IDFC, LTIM, RANBAXY, RPL, SATYAMCOMP, SESAGOA, STER): 12% of member-months in 2008, about 10% in 2009 to 2013, about 2% in 2016 to 2023. This biases results upward. The Midcap list is not a true point-in-time list (12 additions, 1 removal in 7.5 years), so rule R6 never fires there. In 22 months of the Nifty 50 file there are two rows, which are merged.
Limits. The Nifty 50 list is point-in-time only from Jan 2008, with a frozen list for the first 33 months and about 10-12% of member-months without prices in 2008 to 2013; nothing earlier than 2008 can be tested. The Midcap list is mostly Nifty 50 names, 7.4 years, one bull market. Smallcap 50 and Midcap 150 have no usable list. Fills are at the close (a next-open fill costs about 2 points a year). Tax assumes capital-gains treatment; a high-turnover trader may be taxed as business income. Windows overlap, so they are far fewer independent observations than they look. Single full-history runs are one path and swing widely; use the windows.
